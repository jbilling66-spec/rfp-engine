# Success strategies

How to get the most out of this engine. The system is built so that
good inputs and honest reviews compound; these are the habits that make
it pay.

## Feed it well at the start

The quality ceiling of a response is set in the first ten minutes. Give
every pursuit a complete RFP workbook, and always record a ramble —
your voice notes about the deal are the one input only you can supply,
and they steer the brief, the themes, and the plan. A pursuit started
bare produces a technically correct response with no point of view.
If you have buyer research, attach the pack; the engine cites it
rather than guessing.

## Treat the gates as the cheap place to steer

Gate 1 (brief + themes) and Gate 2 (plan) exist because redirecting
one page of strategy costs minutes and redirecting a finished draft
costs an afternoon. Read them like you mean it: a weak theme approved
at Gate 1 is a weak thread through every section after it. Approving
fast is fine; approving blind wastes the checkpoint you paid for.

## Gaps are the system working

When the engine opens a gap instead of writing something, that is the
honesty you bought — it found a slot the knowledge base cannot ground.
Answer gaps with substance and they become permanent knowledge; force
prose past them and you sign your name to a guess. The same goes for
refusals anywhere in the system: a refusal is information about a
missing input, never an obstacle to route around.

## The knowledge base compounds — if you tend it

Every past response you ingest makes the next draft stronger, and
every steward decision teaches the store what good looks like. Work
the proposal queue while context is fresh; a proposal reviewed months
late is reviewed by a stranger. Edit drafts inside the tool rather
than in exported copies — the engine measures which cards survive your
edits and quietly ranks reliable content higher. Record every outcome,
win or loss; unrecorded outcomes teach nothing.

## Review the annotated draft, not just the prose

The annotated draft shows you what every claim stands on. Spend your
attention where the annotations are thin, not where the writing reads
smoothly — fluent prose over a weak citation is exactly the failure
the validator exists to catch. Never talk yourself past a Tier-1
block; either the fact gets verified into the fact sheet or the claim
comes out.

## Watch cost like a metric, not a fear

Every run meters its spend and aborts at its ceiling rather than
surprise you. The board shows cost to date per pursuit; the number is
a run-totals figure you can trust because it is derived from the same
lines the audit trail is. Cheap-and-wrong loses deals; the expensive
habit to avoid is re-running stages to dodge a gap a human should
answer.

## The failure modes to avoid

Starting a pursuit without a ramble. Approving gates without reading.
Answering gaps with "see above." Letting the proposal queue age.
Editing exports instead of the tool. Ingesting a document and never
recording how the deal ended. Each one starves the exact mechanism
that makes the next pursuit better than this one.
